# Changelog

Notable changes to GSS, grouped by release. Features developed across several commits are listed once.

The 0.0.1 history was reconstructed from GitHub.

## Unreleased

### Added

- **`material`**: `toon()`, flat bands of light, like a cartoon. `material: toon` shades an object in flat bands of light, like a cartoon or a cel-shaded game: from half the color in the shade to the full color, with a highlight. `toon(5)` gives it more bands (from 2 to 8, 3 by default), and `toon(#ff5a36)` a color of its own. It takes the sun, the lights of the scene and their shadows, like the other materials.
- **`content`**: a number among the strings, `"item: " sibling-index()`. `content` takes a number, joined to the strings around it like CSS joins strings: `content: "item: " sibling-index()` numbers each copy, `content: var(--i)` writes a variable that holds a number. The number is written like CSS writes it, at most six decimals. The digits of a font are drawn once, so a scene can number many copies (64 copies use 2 of its 16 images). Unlike CSS, where `content` takes no number. A text with a number holds on one line.
- **Docs**: a page that a version gave a new value says "updated in". In the contents of the docs and the "New in" of the search, a page that the newest version gave a new value, like the number of `content`, says `updated in v0.0.8`, beside the pages it added, which say `new in v0.0.8`.
- **`@debug-panel`**: the axes, a grid and everything known about the scene, in the playground. `@debug-panel { display: open; }` draws the x, y and z axes (red, green, blue, each with its letter) and a grid at the origin over the render of the playground and of Try it, fainter behind the objects, and shows a panel with the backend, the camera as the `camera-angle` and `camera-distance` to copy into the code, the objects, the lights, the images used out of 16, the variables, the hover states, the filter passes, the lines of the shader, the mean and the max of the steps per pixel under `view: steps`, and in the playground the frames per second, the time of a frame on the GPU and the time the shader took to build. `display: folded` keeps the axes and folds the panel. A page that embeds the scene shows neither. The VS Code extension colors it.
- **`view`**: `steps` colors each pixel by the work it costs. `view: steps` colors each pixel by how many times the shader measured the scene for it: dark blue for a few, then green, yellow, and red at 150 and over. The red parts are the ones that slow a scene down. A third chip, `view: steps`, switches to it in the playground and in Try it.
- **Home page**: a section that thanks the contributors. The home page thanks the contributors: a section "Contributors" with the avatar and the name of each person whose pull request is in GSS, and what each pull request brought, linked to it. The footer links to it.
- **Changelog**: a page for it on the site, `/changelog`. A page for the changelog, `/changelog`: every version, the newest first, each with its anchor (`/changelog#0.0.7`). Each change is one line that leads to its page in the docs. Beside it, a cloud of points drawn in GSS writes the number of each version as the page scrolls, and scatters between two versions. The version in the footer of every page opens it, where it opened npm.
- **VS Code extension**: the GSS of an HTML page, colored. The editor extension colors GSS written in an HTML page: the content of a `<script type="text/gss">` is highlighted as GSS, the tag around it as HTML.
- **VS Code extension**: completion of property names and mixins. The editor extension completes property names: while you type, the properties the rule can take, with their syntax and description, and the mixins of the file after `@apply`, in a `.gss` file and in a `<script type="text/gss">`.
- **Docs**: `scroll()` and `view()` have pages of their own. `scroll()` and `view()` have pages of their own in the docs, in a group "Scroll timelines" after the easings: the scroller and the axis each one takes, and examples to try. The page of `animation-timeline` still lists them.
- **Brand page**: the films of GSS, to play or to download. The films of GSS on the brand page: the teaser, the launch film and the films of 0.0.6 and 0.0.7, to play in place or to download in 16:9 or 9:16.

### Fixed

- **`:hover`**: each rule keeps its own hover state. An object with several `:hover` rules that wait for different objects no longer takes all of them as soon as one is hovered: `.point:hover { scale: 2; }` scales the hovered point only, while `#cloud:has(.point:hover) .point { … }` moves every point. When several apply at once, the most specific wins, like CSS. The copies of `* n` no longer share their hover either.
- **`filter`**: the text or the image of an object no longer stays blank under `bloom()` or `blur()`. With `bloom()` or `blur()` in the `filter` of the scene, the text of `content` or the image of `texture` on the last object could stay blank once the scene stopped moving: the image of the filter took its place. Each object now reads its own image, at every size of the canvas.

## [0.0.7] — 2026-10-23

### Added

- **`@property-panel`**: `range` gives a slider its ends and its step. `range` in `@property-panel`: the scene gives the slider of a variable its ends and its step. `@property-panel { range: --col 0 3 1, --row 0 3 1; }` makes two sliders from 0 to 3, one whole number at a time, for a cube on a board of 4 by 4. A number typed past an end is held there, and the panel says what the end is; a variable without a range keeps its slider as before.
- **Attribute selectors**: `[attr]`, `[attr=value]` and the other forms of CSS. Attribute selectors, like CSS: `[attr]`, `[attr=value]`, `~=`, `|=`, `^=`, `$=`, `*=`, and the flags `i` and `s`. An object gets its attributes in `@scene`, like HTML: `@scene { cube[data-col=2]; sphere[lit]; }`, then `[data-col=2] { color: red; }`. Its id and its classes count as the attributes `id` and `class`, so `[id^="hero-"]` styles every copy of `torus#hero * 3`. An attribute weighs like a class.
- **`texture-mapping`**: `sphere` wraps an image around the object, like a globe. `texture-mapping: sphere` wraps the image of `texture` around the object, like a map around a globe: a map of the Earth, the Moon or a planet, twice as wide as it is tall, covers a sphere in one piece. The middle of the image faces the front, its top is the top, and its left and right edges meet at the back. `box`, the default, keeps the image on each face, as before. The docs have the Earth and the Moon to try, on maps by NASA.
- **`content`**: text on the surface of any object. `content`: a text on the surface of an object. `cube { content: "hello"; }` writes the word on the faces of the cube: it turns and takes the light with it, and goes behind what stands in front. The text is as large as each face lets it be, and `cube::face(front) { content: "1"; }` gives one face its own, for a die or a sign. On a round shape the text wraps around it like a label on a can; a pyramid and an octahedron write on each of their faces; any other shape takes it like a sticker. It reads a variable (`content: var(--word)`), and takes its font from `font-family`, `font-weight` and `font-style`, like CSS, and its color from `text-color`; without one it is black on a light object and white on a dark one. `"one\A two"` breaks a line, like CSS; `font-size: 0.3` sets the height of the letters in the units of the scene and breaks the lines at the width of the face, and `text-align` sets their side. An emoji keeps its colors. On `:hover` and in `@keyframes`, `text-color` and `font-size` change smoothly, and the text and its font change halfway, like CSS. Unlike CSS, `content` goes on the object itself, not on a `::before`.
- **`::face()`**: a face by its number, like `:nth-child()`. `::face()` takes a number, like `:nth-child()`: `::face(3)`, `::face(odd)`, `::face(n + 5)`. A cube is numbered like a die (front 1, top 2, right 3, left 4, bottom 5, back 6), a pyramid from its front slope turning right then its base, an octahedron from its front-right top triangle.
- **`mix-blend-mode`**: an object blends with what is behind it, like CSS. `mix-blend-mode` on objects, like CSS: an object blends its color with what is behind it, the objects behind it, the floor and the background. `sphere { mix-blend-mode: multiply; }` tints what shows through the sphere; three spheres with `screen` add up to white where they meet. Every mode of `background-blend-mode`, and `plus-lighter`. It works with `opacity`, on a group (each of its objects takes the mode), on `:hover` and in `@keyframes`, where it changes from one mode to the other halfway through, like CSS.
- **`@mixin`, `@apply`**: mixins, like CSS. Mixins, like CSS: `@mixin --name { … }` names a block of declarations and rules, and `@apply --name;` puts it in any rule — an object, `:hover`, a nested rule, a `@media`, even a frame of `@keyframes`. A mixin takes parameters with defaults, read with `var()` (`@mixin --ball(--size: 0.4) { radius: var(--size); }`, then `@apply --ball(0.7);`), and `@contents` places the block given to `@apply`, inside a `@media` for example. Close to the `@mixin` and `@include` of Sass, written the way CSS writes it. The scene compiles as if the mixin were written out in each rule, and a mistake (an unknown mixin, a value too many, a mixin that applies itself) is an error that points at it.
- **`@for`, `@each`**: loops, like Sass, written with CSS variables. Loops, `@for` and `@each`, like Sass, written with CSS variables. `@for --i from 1 through 4 { … }` writes its block once for each number, with `var(--i)` replaced by it; `to` leaves the last number out, `by` sets the step, and the numbers can take a unit (`from 0deg to 360deg by 45deg`). `@each --x in tomato, gold, teal { … }` does the same for each value of a list. A loop goes anywhere: in `@scene` to make objects, even of a different shape at each step (`@each --shape in cube, sphere, cone { var(--shape); }`), around rules, where `var(--i)` works in the selector too (`.step:nth-child(var(--i))`), in a rule, in `@keyframes`, in a mixin, in another loop for a grid. The scene compiles as if each step were written by hand, and a mistake (no `from`, a step of 0, more than 1000 steps) is an error that points at it.
- **`controls`**: the gestures that move the camera, on the scene or on an object. `controls`: the gestures that move the camera, on `scene` or on an object. `scene { controls: none; }` keeps the camera where the GSS puts it and lets the wheel scroll the page; `controls: orbit` keeps the drag, `controls: zoom` the wheel. On an object, `controls: none` lets it be pressed without turning the camera, while a drag from the floor still turns it: the pieces of Back Rank and the buttons of Midnight Arcade, in the playground, now hold still. Like `cursor`, a group passes it to its objects, and `@media` can change it. On a page, `<gss-scene controls="none">` still turns every gesture off.
- **Docs on GitHub**: the reference as Markdown pages in `docs/`. Docs on GitHub: the reference of gss-lang.dev/docs as Markdown pages in `docs/`, one per section, from Getting started and Installation to every property, with an index. The README links to them, so the repository is enough to start with GSS.
- **Playground**: four scenes for the features of 0.0.6. Playground: four scenes for the features of 0.0.6, in a group "New in 0.0.6" at the top of the examples. Midnight Arcade: two cabinets after closing time, one screen drawn by a `@paint` shader, the other a game of Pong in a `<canvas>` shown with `element()`, neon in `emissive()` under `bloom()`, a flickering sign, the lens of `vignette()` and `chromatic-aberration()`, coins that drop into their slot on a click. Back Rank: a checkmate on a `checker()` board, with pyramids, tubes and octahedrons in silver and copper, outlines on hover, and the king in check in a red `double` outline. Lighthouse: scroll to build it, then to light it, four parts of the way of the scene across the page, set by `animation-range` on `view()`. Bubble Works: a copper machine that blows `iridescent` bubbles to pop, `stripes()` climbing a glass pipe, and gauges in the shaded styles of `outline`. The scenes of 0.0.4 and 0.0.5 move to a group of their own.
- **Showcase**: an archive of every scene, with its version and its features. Showcase: an archive of every scene of the page, the studies included, in place of the inspiration grid. Each scene has its capture, its name, what it is, a badge of the version of GSS it came with, and the features it uses, each linked to its page of the docs. Newest first, as a grid of cards or as a list; the page remembers the choice.

- **`@scene`**: shapes, copies with `* n`, and groups. Declare shapes in `@scene`, multiply them with `* n`, and nest them in groups with inherited transforms.
- **Selectors**: shape, class, id, universal, list and descendant. Style objects with shape, class, ID, universal, list and descendant selectors; resolve specificity, source order and `!important`.
- **Shapes**: cubes, spheres, tori, cylinders, cones, capsules, planes, paths and prisms. Draw cubes, spheres, tori, cylinders, cones, capsules, planes, SVG path tubes and extruded prisms, including SVG arcs and filled contours.
- **Transforms**: translate, rotate, scale, and combinations of shapes. Translate, rotate and scale objects; combine their distance fields with union, subtraction, intersection and blending.
- **`material`**: matte, metal, jelly and glass. Use matte, metal, jelly and glass materials, including reflections, refraction and frost styles.
- **`@keyframes`**: animations computed in the shader. Animate translation, rotation, scale and color with `@keyframes`, computed in the shader.
